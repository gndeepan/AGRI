## Stem borer
At the vegetative stage, larvae feeding inside the stem cause "dead hearts": the central shoot dries and pulls out easily. At the reproductive stage the damage shows as "white ears": empty, whitish panicles.

## Leaf folder
Larvae fold leaves lengthwise and scrape the green tissue, leaving white streaks. Damage is usually higher in heavily fertilized fields.

## Brown planthopper (BPH)
Brown planthoppers gather at the base of the plants near the water line. Heavy infestation causes "hopperburn": circular patches of plants that turn yellow, then brown, and dry out. Too much nitrogen and close spacing favour BPH. Check the base of the hills by tilting the plants.

## Blast and sheath blight
Blast causes spindle-shaped lesions with grey centres on leaves, and lesions on the panicle neck ("neck blast"). Sheath blight causes oval, greenish-grey lesions on the leaf sheaths near the water line, which spread upward in dense canopies.

## Scouting practice
Walk the field in a zig-zag and check several hills at random each week. Record the stage, the symptoms and the share of plants affected. Decide on control only with advice from the Agricultural Officer or KVK, based on economic threshold levels.
