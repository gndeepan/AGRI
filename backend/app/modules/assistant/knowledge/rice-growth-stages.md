## Three growth phases
Rice growth is divided into the vegetative phase (germination to panicle initiation), the reproductive phase (panicle initiation to flowering) and the ripening phase (flowering to maturity). In the tropics the reproductive phase takes about 35 days and ripening about 30 days. Differences in total duration between varieties come mainly from the length of the vegetative phase.

## Tillering
Tillering begins once the seedling is established and continues until about panicle initiation. Tillers that form late often do not produce panicles.

## Panicle initiation and booting
Panicle initiation marks the start of the reproductive phase. The young panicle grows inside the stem, then the boot (flag-leaf sheath) swells. Water or nutrient stress here reduces the number of grains.

## Flowering
Heading is followed by anthesis (flowering), which usually occurs in the morning. High temperature (above about 35 °C) at anthesis can cause spikelet sterility.

## Ripening
Grains pass through milk, dough and mature stages. A crop is generally ready to harvest when about 80–85% of the grains are straw-coloured.
